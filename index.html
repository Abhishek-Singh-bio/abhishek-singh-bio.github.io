Research

How does genome maintenance cross into genome instability?

We study the molecular events in between biological pathways and disease outcomes: protein binding, sliding-clamp formation, DNA topology, repetitive DNA structure, and repair-complex assembly.

Abhishek Kumar Singh, Ph.D.

Molecular biophysicist | DNA repair | Single-molecule methods

Dr. Abhishek Kumar Singh works at the interface of DNA repair, single-molecule biophysics, and biomolecular spectroscopy. His research focuses on how DNA repair proteins recognize, assemble on, and move along structurally complex DNA substrates, particularly repetitive DNA sequences associated with repeat expansion diseases.

His scientific background connects relaxation dynamics, dielectric and terahertz spectroscopy, biomolecular hydration, magnetic tweezers, fluorescence microscopy, and quantitative single-molecule analysis.

The long-term vision of the Molecular Biophysics & Genome Stability Lab is to uncover how molecular motion, DNA mechanics, protein assembly, and biomolecular interfaces regulate DNA repair outcomes.

Methods

Tools for watching molecules in motion.

Our approach integrates force, fluorescence, spectroscopy, instrumentation, and computation to connect molecular dynamics with genome stability.

Magnetic tweezers

Force and torque-based measurements of DNA mechanics, protein-DNA interactions, and dynamic molecular states.

Single-molecule fluorescence

TIRF imaging and fluorescence-based assays to visualize repair proteins, DNA substrates, and molecular assemblies.

Flow-stretching assays

High-throughput DNA visualization and trajectory analysis for protein diffusion, confinement, and DNA structure studies.

Instrumentation

Open and accessible approaches for microscope control, real-time image acquisition, and bead tracking.

Spectroscopy

Terahertz and dielectric spectroscopy approaches to probe hydration, relaxation, and biomolecular interfaces.

Quantitative analysis

Python/MATLAB workflows for image processing, single-molecule trajectories, statistical analysis, and modeling.
